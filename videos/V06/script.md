## THE EDIT STYLE: "the creator dashboard"

Same art and the same Remotion phone kit as V05, with a new frame on top:

1. **The Creator Dashboard HUD** (top left, replaces V05's Debt Tracker) shows `FOLLOWERS 10.4K · EARNED $1,250 · KEPT $610`.
   - FOLLOWERS counts up with a soft tick.
   - EARNED is gold.
   - KEPT is green, and it is always smaller. The gap between EARNED and KEPT is the visual thread of the video.
2. **Level cards are milestone notifications.** A generic badge pops up ("🎉 You hit 10,000 followers"), confetti falls, and the level number slams in. No platform logos and no play buttons.
3. **The brand DM inbox** is a running gag. DMs from invented brands ("GlowSip", "Vexo Energy", "SnugBox") get longer, more professional and better paid each level. At Level 1 it's "collab? 💕". At Level 7 it's a PDF contract.
4. **The "Where the dollar goes" bar.** At the big deals, a stacked bar splits the payment into platform, manager, editor, tax and *you*.
5. **The phone shows the content.** SU01's own posts appear in an invented feed. Likes and comments swarm, then the screen cuts to his banking app, which never matches the likes.

**Visuals:** library plates, poses and cast. One new prop sheet (listed at the end). Everything else is code.

### Short A: "What 1 million views actually pays" (33 s)
You got a million views. How much did you make?

On YouTube, you only keep fifty-five percent of the ad money on regular videos, and forty-five percent of your share on Shorts. The platform keeps the rest.

So a million views on a Short might pay… tens to a few hundred dollars. The same views on a long video could pay thousands. Same views. A completely different check.

That's why creators who look famous are often broke, and why the smart ones sell their own stuff.

So next time you see a video with a million views, ask the real question…

*[Loops back to "You got a million views."]*

### Short B: "The free hoodie trap" (29 s)
A brand sends you a free hoodie. Congrats… you might owe taxes on it.

In the U.S., products you get in exchange for promotion generally count as income, at what they'd cost to buy. And if you post about it, the FTC says you have to disclose it, right there in the post. Not in your bio.

Ten free hoodies a month? That's real income on paper… and not one dollar in your bank.

So the next time a DM says "can we send you something?", remember what really happens when…

*[Loops back to "…a brand sends you a free hoodie."]*

## RESEARCH AND EDITORIAL NOTES (NOT SPOKEN)

**Angle:** a level-by-level POV following one creator from 0 to 10M. The hook asks "at which level do you actually make a living?". The payoff answers it: Level 8 (own something). 100K is the trap and 1M is "a well-paid job". The EARNED vs KEPT gap is the visual thread from start to end.

**Facts used (all checked on 7 Oct 2026):**
- **YouTube YPP:**
  - Lower tier: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - Full tier: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
  - Source: YouTube Help, "Overview of the expanded YouTube Partner Program". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240
- **Revenue share:** creators get 55% of net watch-page ad revenue, and 45% of revenue allocated from the Shorts Creator Pool. Source: YouTube Help, "YouTube partner earnings overview". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72902
- **Rate ranges per post:** nano (1K–10K) $100–$500, micro (10K–100K) $500–$2,500, macro (100K–1M) $2,500–$10,000+, mega (1M+) $10,000–$50,000+.
  - Source: Later, "Influencer Pricing Benchmarks: The Complete 2026 Guide" (Jul 7, 2026). https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/

Year three. Now, you've probably heard that seventy percent of lottery winners go broke. It's everywhere online.

But the organization that statistic is usually pinned on says it isn't their research, and they can't confirm it.

There's a real study, though. And it's scarier.

Researchers followed about thirty-five thousand lottery winners in Florida. They compared people who won fifty to a hundred and fifty thousand dollars with people who won less than ten thousand.

Right after winning, the bigger winners were less likely to go bankrupt. Makes sense: they had cash.

But three to five years later, the advantage was gone. The money had mostly delayed bankruptcy instead of preventing it. The bigger winners didn't use the win to pay off their debts or build up savings.

To be fair, those were much smaller wins than yours. But the pattern is the point: a windfall doesn't fix money habits. It just gives them more fuel.

And year three is when you feel it too. The excitement's gone. You're a bit bored. You start to wonder why you're living like a normal person when you have millions.

You don't buy the watch. You don't buy the bigger house. That's the hardest decision of the whole five years, and nobody will ever congratulate you for it.

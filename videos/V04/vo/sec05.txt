Level five. Ten thousand dollars a year.

That's real money. For a lot of people, that's a holiday, a car repair fund and an emergency fund, all at once.

It's also a second job.

Evenings: gone. Weekends: gone. Your friends: "Are you… still alive?"

Think about the hours. A week has one hundred and sixty-eight of them. Take off sleep. Take off your day job. Take off commuting, eating, showering and just… existing. What's left is maybe a few hours a day. And now your hustle wants all of them.

And here's the cruel part. At your day job, when you work harder, you get tired. At your side hustle, when you work harder, you get tired and you get more orders. Success makes the problem worse.

This is the time wall. It's where a huge number of side hustles quietly die. The idea wasn't bad. The customers were real. The hustler just… ran out of hours.

The people who break through usually do three things.

One: they raise their prices. It feels terrifying. But if you're fully booked, you're probably too cheap.

Two: they drop the customers who take the most time for the least money. Every hustle has a few. You know exactly who they are.

Three: they automate or outsource the boring stuff. Templates, scheduling tools, a freelancer for the jobs they hate.

At level five, the whole game changes. You stop selling your time… and start building a system.

Level ten. Half a million dollars a year. You've hired people.

And here's the plot twist nobody warns you about. The thing you loved doing? You barely do it anymore.

Your job now is payroll. Hiring. Training. Managing. And fixing whatever broke today. You started a business so you'd never have a boss… and now you are one.

So who do you hire first? A lot of owners say the same thing: hire for the job you're worst at, or the one you hate most. Great at making the product but awful at emails? Your first hire answers the emails. Every hour you spend on the thing you're bad at is an hour you're not spending on the thing only you can do.

And this is where you learn the most important lesson in business. Revenue… is not profit.

Made-up example: five hundred thousand dollars comes in. Staff, stock, software, rent and taxes take three hundred and fifty thousand of it. What's actually yours is a hundred and fifty.

Still a great result. But it's not what the level bar says. So next time someone online says "My business made half a million this year"… ask them one question. Made? Or kept?
